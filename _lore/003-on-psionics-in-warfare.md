# On Psionics in Warfare

Compiled from the notes of Tactics Consultant, Blute Yungen

The usage of Psionics in modern combat is a complication to the classic Atomic Promise. The Atomic Promise boils down to a very simple principle: you kick me, I kick you just as hard, if not a little harder. Merely ‘kicking’ is not exactly within the Psionic arsenal, and from the data presented to me it appears as though the Hashetty have taken zero interest in learning how to just throw a good roundhouse like the rest of us. Say you kick a Psionic army, in response they’ll only do a lot of yelling and fancy hand waving. Sometimes you’ll get magically slapped like you expect, but sometimes nothing will happen and it’ll appear pointless, silly even, especially when considering the nasty bruise you gave them. But soon after you’ll miraculously discover that your boyfriend has dumped you and begun dating your mother, who as it happened has also turned blue. It is for this reason that I recommend ceasing the usage of all large-scale atomic weaponry against the Hashetty, and engage in negotiations regarding their usage of Psionics on any scale.

While their Psionic attacks have been proportional to our own actions (often less destructive even), we are still stuck untangling the consequences of our earliest engagements with them. Below I will break down a few of the larger events to illustrate how even the most innocuous Psionics outclass our best tactics and weaponry:

## The Hemo-Shuffling of the Sagittarius Fleet
*[Ritual-cast Atomic Scale Psionic Incident]*

While initially thought to be a form of psychosis or illness, this Psionic blast was eventually identified as being responsible for completely randomizing the blood-types of everything within range. This lead to over one hundred thousand deaths on its own because the soldiers’ specially tailored combat drugs became unable to properly bond with their altered cells. These deaths were often slow and could have been prevented quite easily provided there were enough dialysis machines. Blood transfusions became impossible as well as each blood bag needed to be re-identified and catalogued, which introduced further complications. Outside of the typical ABO range, the blood types of H, T, 7, and G were discovered and classified by various medical crews during their retreat. The scope of this was not fully realized until many of the seemingly unaffected soldiers and staff were released, only to later develop an array of new, and frequently terminal blood disorders.

The combination of physical effects on staff, accompanied by the long-running psychological fallout lead to the slow collapse of the entirety of the Sagittarius Fleet. Superstition surrounding the event, as well as genuine medical concern all but halted the system’s birthrate long after the fleet was put on temporary leave. Emigration and desertion rose dramatically in the following months and the system soon needed an infusion of personnel that was over several years away from being ready. Once it became clear that the fleet would never get back on its feet again, it was already far too late and their system was captured in a surrender to avoid further loss of life.

By comparison, it is worth noting that the Exo-Planet Janus, the glassing of which provoked such a response, had a population of 43,648. While tactically advantageous, it possessed no noteworthy resource hotspots or unique features.

## The Coupling of Commander Gashoman and Mrs. Tilly
*[Psion-cast Psionic Assassination Attempt (further analysis needed)]*

Gashoman was serving as the commanding officer of Destraven Fleet when his command ship was boarded by a Hashetty breaching squadron lead by a single Psion. The group was quickly dispatched, and it was believed that the Psion had not done any lasting damage (aside from reactor 5A, which was turned upside down). Gashoman celebrated surviving such a close encounter (10km separation) with a Psion, and taunted them on video-screens as they were being apprehended before continuing the assault as planned. No immediate problems presented themselves, though as the campaign continued it became clear that Gashoman was growing progressively more incompetent and temperamental. He continually passed psychological exams and other physical tests, often with better health than his fellows, but the problems persisted. An official investigation into his behavior began when he dispatched a fighter squadron against a Hashetty ambassador who he said talked to him like he was “a little sniveling bitch.”

An initial round of the investigation turned over an odd stone, Mrs. Tilly. She was picked out by a pattern-finding algorithm that logged her recent complaints of unexpected mood swings as being quite similar to Gashoman’s symptoms (previously written off as a case of bipolar disorder, despite no family history). She had been aboard an evacuation craft that was docking around the same time as the Psion’s boarding party, the data point that ultimately lead the algorithm to report her to investigators. She proved equally as healthy as Gashoman, and it was only when putting them in a room together that things became clear. The appointment was late in the day, just before Mrs. Tilly’s husband was set to come home from work. Naturally, she was thinking about little else when she came in. Gashoman, quite conversely, became loathed to the company of others, and sneered at a medical technician who he believed had been flirting with him. When given emotional stimuli, the other person appeared to spontaneously exhibit the ‘opposite’ feeling, and would switch between emotions wildly if the two people experienced conflicting things at the same time. Simple as that.

While there were many safeguards in place to ensure that no form of mind control or coercion had hold of Commander Gashoman, they failed to detect anything. Mrs. Tilly’s influence on him mapped perfectly with predictive models for human emotional fluctuations (she is human herself after all), and even in retrospect it is difficult to fully identify her influence when looking at Gashoman’s brain-scans. At the time it was simply the common sentiment that “he’d been having a rough couple days, didn’t think anything of it.” Several thousand lives were lost as a result.

To set things right, Gashoman was executed for engaging a peaceful entity unprovoked. In an attempt to preserve Mrs. Tilly’s wellbeing, Dr. Fjord shot Gashoman through the temple as he was still waking up in an attempt to catch him during a state of mental neutrality. Mrs. Tilly seems to have developed a minor case of insomnia as a result, which she treats with medication.
