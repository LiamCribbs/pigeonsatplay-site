# The Origins of Saxonite

`Abridged Excerpt from Saxon’s 112th Annual Scientific Report`

Hello gals and pals, it’s that time of year again…

Figuring out where Saxonite comes from, how it forms, and how it functions are the physics department’s three main goals (even if the Bio heads will tell you that we’re also trying to figure out how to eat it, which we’re not… anymore).

My team has made incredible headway in unlocking more of Saxonite’s utility, but we’re still saddled with one singular, very unfortunate shame: We are nowhere closer to a method of producing it than we were last year.

A lot of us have thrown in the towel on this problem. It’s just how resources work. No matter how amazing this one is, we’re gonna run out of it at some point, simple as that. That answer has always bothered me though, because for the longest time, the same was true for diamonds. Saxonite isn’t its own element strictly speaking, so scientifically there should be a way for us to fabricate it, even if it might be terribly inefficient. This is one of those ‘blinker thinker’ ideas to me, a real ‘vodka-cran and a xan’ type thing, so it felt right to wrap the year up with some of my musings on it.

Firstly, we know that Saxonite is a fourth-dimensional *and* quantum substance. (For you electrical engineers this means that it can futz with gravity, time, *and* be in two places at once.) The issue we’re faced with now is that Saxonite can seemingly occur anywhere, but only shows up in very high volumes. So here’s my thought: There were once Saxonite planets (or probably just very large asteroids).

It’s a simple idea more or less, Saxonite, being the only fourth-dimensional substance, had free reign to float around the galaxy while the universe got itself together. Just like we wouldn’t be threatened by a little gremlin jumping around on a piece of paper, these Saxonite planets likely floated *around* anything that might’ve broken them apart by drifting in and out of the fourth dimension. This, as I see it, is the only explanation for how Saxonite embedded itself within other planets. So what changed? Why is all the Saxonite stuck now? And why can’t it wander around freely? Because of *us.*

While we’re around to observe the universe, Saxonite cannot be in its unstable, wiggly jiggly state because we force reality to be the single way we see it. So, the first organism to develop any kind of observational capability (probably some little squid asshole with eyes), sent out a massive ‘reality check’ through the universe traveling at the speed of light, which locked all the Saxonite in place. The stuff that was floating *through* planets became a part of that planet, and I imagine there was a time at which many planets had large Saxonite fractals that grew off of them. My guess is that any free-floating Saxonite was left to decay, explode, or perhaps without any third-dimensional matter to attach to, it was forced to slip back into the fourth dimension, and out of our grubby little hands…

Anywho, I’m off to Mars to eat sponge cake. *Ta ta!*
