# On Psionics in Warfare, Excerpt 1

`Compiled from the notes of Tactics Consultant Blute Yungen`

The usage of Psionics in modern combat is a complication to the classic Atomic Promise. The Atomic Promise boils down to a very simple principle: you kick me, I kick you just as hard, if not a little harder. Merely ‘kicking’ is not exactly within the Psionic arsenal, and from the data presented to me it appears as though the Hashetty have taken zero interest in learning how to just throw a good roundhouse like the rest of us. Say you kick a Psionic soldier, in response they’ll only do a lot of yelling and fancy hand waving. Sometimes you’ll get magically slapped like you expect, but sometimes nothing will happen and it’ll appear pointless, silly even, especially when considering the nasty bruise you gave them. But soon after you’ll miraculously discover that your boyfriend has dumped you and begun dating your mother, who as it happened has also turned blue. It is for this reason that I recommend ceasing the usage of all large-scale atomic weaponry against the Hashetty, and engage in negotiations regarding their usage of Psionics on any scale.

While their Psionic attacks have been proportional to our own actions (often less destructive even), we are still stuck untangling the consequences of our earliest engagements with them. Below I will break down a few of the larger events to illustrate how even the most innocuous Psionics outclass our best tactics and weaponry:

## **The Hemo-Shuffling of the Sagittarius Fleet**

*[Ritual-cast Atomic Scale Psionic Incident]*

While initially thought to be a form of psychosis or illness, this Psionic blast was eventually identified as being responsible for completely randomizing the blood-types of everything within range. This led to over one hundred thousand deaths on its own because the soldiers’ specially tailored combat drugs became unable to properly bond with their altered cells. These deaths were often slow and could have been prevented quite easily provided there were enough dialysis machines. Blood transfusions became impossible as well, as each blood bag needed to be re-identified and catalogued, which introduced further complications. Outside of the typical ABO range, the blood types of H, T, 7, and G were discovered and classified by various medical crews during their retreat. The scope of this was not fully realized until many of the seemingly unaffected soldiers and staff were released, only to later develop an array of new, and frequently terminal blood disorders.

The combination of physical effects on staff accompanied by the long-running psychological fallout led to the slow collapse of the entirety of the Sagittarius Fleet. Superstition surrounding the event – as well as genuine medical concern – all but halted the system’s birthrate long after the fleet was put on temporary leave. Emigration and desertion rose dramatically in the following months and the system soon needed an infusion of personnel that was over several years away from being ready. Once it became clear that the fleet would never get back on its feet again, it was already far too late and their system was captured in a surrender to avoid further loss of life.

By comparison, it is worth noting that the Exo-Planet Janus, the glassing of which provoked such a response, had a population of 43,648. While tactically advantageous, it possessed no noteworthy resource hotspots or unique features.
