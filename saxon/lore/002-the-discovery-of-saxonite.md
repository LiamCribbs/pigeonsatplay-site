# The Discovery of Saxonite

His arrival is ceremonious, right up until he speaks.

The team of scientists immediately erupt into applause, wooping and cheering as the transport ships door folds downward into the blue-tinted snow. This sort of celebration is typically very disingenuous when it comes to CEOs, a shallow commendation for someone — who is otherwise useless — being in the right place, at the right time, with the right amount of money… but not here. This is the man that flushed everything he had down the drain to support their ideas because he believed in something better. Ill-advised or not, his unwavering support of their long-shot idea has lead right up to this triumphant moment. The moment when the past ends, and the future begins.

“Are you always this fucking loud?” He asks, the cane wavering slightly in his white-knuckled hand. Anyone who was still murmuring falls silent and his eyebrows flash in a small sign of approval. “As you were.” He says, but everyone stays where they are since him being here means all that’s left to do is stand here looking at him. He rolls his eyes after a few of the scientists in the front rows shuffle noncommittally, shifting their attention away from the surprisingly intimidating geriatric. The crowd watches in silence as a team of aids fit him into a metal exoskeleton that, once turned on, allows him to toss the cane aside and stride down the ramp with the energy of a man easily one third his age. The dark metal of the device blends seamlessly into his black suit, only visible when it catches the light at odd angles, or overlaps the crimson trimming.

Hamhock Saxon steps down into the snow and sneezes almost immediately, the servos in his suit whining as his body lurches unexpectedly. He annoyedly wipes his nose on his sleeve, grimaces, then turns his attention to the crowd, scanning their ranks as he rubs his hands together. Dr. Foster, the only man he knows, steps forward from the front row and Hamhock gives him an old man’s warm smile, beckoning him forward. The two men shake hands and Hamhock pulls him into a hug before whispering to him.

“So… Now that I’m here, it’s really that big a deal, isn’t it?”

Dr. Foster pulls back from him, looking over his boss for a second before giving a single, curt nod. Hamhock sighs, in relief or exhaustion it’s hard to tell, then pumps his fist up into the air.

“There ya go you beautiful people! You did it!” The crowd properly erupts now, only cooling down once the gathered scientists begin digging into the food and drinks that Hamhock’s aids unload from his transport. His own excitement, only for show, drops away as he walks with Foster off to the side, where the two other team-leads and their assistants are waiting for them. The CEO greets them all in turn, giving each half-compliments on their work, talking about things that he doesn’t fully understand which he doesn’t try to hide. The group walk silently over to the dig site, an oval marked with a dozen little yellow flags. Snow within the area glows a light green, an unnatural color that makes it look like a large TV screen has been buried underneath. Next to the zone, a single wood-handled pickaxe leans casually against a snowmobile. Hamhock hefts the tool easily, tossing it from hand to hand as he stands just outside of the glowing ground.

“It’s right under here then?”

“Might take a few swings, but it’s there.”

“Huh…” He settles the pickaxe in his hands. Behind them, the din of the party continues, the shadows of people dancing and enjoying themselves flickering across the snow as a large bonfire is constructed at the center of camp.

“…I wouldn’t wait to long.” Foster, the radiologist, says after several minutes. “The radiation… it doesn’t stop.”

“I was wondering why we they didn’t stuff me in the lead pajamas.” Hamhock chuckles. “But what do you mean exactly? It doesn’t stop?”

“That’s how we found this place.” Dr. Tanner whistles. “We thought the beams were coming from the planet we were on, couple thousand light years off that way.” She waves in a random direction. “But it was punching *through* the planet, from all the way out here…” She waves at the ground now. “It doesn’t stop, not for anything.” Hamhock’s jaw works for a second before he says:

“Which as I believe you said,” He nods to Foster. “Requires more energy than is physically possible, and a structure that is similarly… impossible.” He nods to Dr. Still and the geologist gives a wan smile. “But it’s right here…”

He lifts the pickaxe above his head, standing picturesque for a moment as he prepares to change history. Just as it seems like he’s about to bring it down, he scowls and tosses the tool back off to the side. The scientists stare at him in disbelief and he only shrugs, giving each of them a look that says little beyond ‘what can ya do.’ He takes a deep breath, making eye contact with each of the leads, then all of their assistants.

“Alright then.” He says. “Which one of you is the spy?” They all stand in a stunned silence, unease growing throughout the small crowd as they begin furtively glancing at each other. “It’s one of you.” He continues. “Not you three,” He waves at the leads. “And not you, hello Anna, I know we didn’t get a chance to talk but I remember you from the barbecue. But anyway, that means it’s… No not you, that wouldn’t make much sense.” He waves dismissively at a chemist standing behind Dr. Tanner. “Ah, yes it must be you. You’re exactly their style.” He levels a finger and the crowd quickly parts around a short, thin woman with neatly tied hair who had previously been nestled in with the rest of the geologists.

She looks up in confusion, but seeing the determination in his face it quickly shifts to into pure hatred. Without warning she backpedals out of the crowd, her right hand flashing up to the hem of her jacket. She pinches the fabric between her thumb and forefinger and something within makes a loud click, followed by a sinister electronic buzzing. Her hand drops down to her side like she’s about to draw from a holster, and in an eye blink the area under her palm blazes with a flickering black and blue light that explodes outwards with a sound like popping lightning. Her hand zips down in a practiced motion that catches the summoned pistol before it drops, shimmering remnants of the teleport burning into her glove as she sweeps the pistol up towards Hamhock. The CEO lunges straight toward her with uncanny speed, the exoskeleton shrieking as his fingers wrap around the barrel of the gun and wrench it away from him. There’s a sharp crack as the weapon fires skyward, followed by a jamming crunch of aluminum as his grip around the slide halts the cycling mechanism. He grunts in satisfaction, then uses his assisted strength to pull the steaming pistol away from her before tossing it off into the snow.

Several of the scientists, many of whom had very clearly been her friends just a few moments ago, tackle her to the ground. She thrashes and screams the whole way down, staring death at the CEO who only returns an impassive expression. While she cools down he casually examines his hand, calling for some medical supplies as blood streams out from his thin, pale skin.

“God, let her go.” He says once he’s done tending to his cuts, almost laughing at how many people it’s taking to hold her down. “You think she’s gonna summon a tank?”

The spy pulls up to her feet, dusting off her jacket before staring defiantly around at all of them.

“If it’s any consolation, you’ve gotten the closest.” He says. “Would’ve had me if you played it a little different. But no… you wanna see what’s under here.” She doesn’t say anything, only simmers there with the same ire she started with. “Well you will, and you’re getting promoted. It’s actually quite convenient you’re here, saves me a lot of jabbering with your commanders.” He points a finger into his mouth in disgust.

“You’re planning on negotiating with me?”

“Oh?” He looks at her in surprise. “Normally they don’t talk. Well perfect, this will be much easier.” He pats her on the shoulder and she punches him hard in the ribs, knocking him over into the snow. Over the course of the next five minutes, the spy is tackled again, released, and let back onto her feet. Now she stands before Hamhock again, this time with a few more bruises, so he continues. “No dear, I’m not negotiating with you. You’re my new emissary to the Federation, you’ll like that a lot more. First perk is you get to live.”

“Joy.” She scoffs.

“No one likes a sore loser.” He tuts. “Plus, you guys brought this on yourselves. Now where was I? Right, ground work. You’re quite deep cover, so are you a real geologist?”

She shakes her head and several of the scientists let out groans, a few even begin tearing up at the betrayal while others fire off insults, but Hamhock only smiles wryly.

“I always love you guys. Somehow no one’s got a fuckin clue there’s somethin up until you pull the gun.” He chuckles. “Well they liked you alright, so I’m guessing you’re not a retard. Gimme an estimate then, how much trouble could I get into with that?” He waves generally to the green glowing pool next to them.

“…A planet.” She says after a second. “At least.”

“I’ve been told the same, and the ground’s full of this stuff.” He says. “And it’s mine, you see. This whole dumb ceremony is just so I can pull this green rock outta the ground, take some pictures, be a scientific pioneer for all of two hours, and give it a stupid ass name…” He hacks out a cough. ”Saxonite’s the current favorite, just to keep everything ‘on brand,’ you know. Then boom… we’re a galactic threat.”

“Mm,” The spy raises a finger. “I’m getting shipped off for… what? To tell the council that you’re ready to pop a couple planets if we don’t comply?”

“Pop a couple- God no.” He lets out a big laugh. “Are you fucking kidding? I own an airline company, what are we gonna do without those planets? I’ll tell ya, fly around in fuckin circles till we get bored enough to blow our brains out.” He takes out a pack of cigarettes and pulls one out for himself before offering one to the spy. She declines. “No, I’m going to be giving you my conditions, and you’re going to have the likely very unpleasant job of convincing all of those council chairs to accept all of them… No questions asked.”

“Shoot me.” She says. “Then send them an email.”

“Hah! And to think, people are gonna say you were the good guys.” He sighs. “I had a lot of time to think about this on the way over, and I’m pretty set on my terms. But before that, let’s see what all the fuss is about.” He finally wraps his hand around the handle of the pickaxe, carrying the tool out into the middle of the pool where he raises it above is head before bringing it down as hard as he can.

The spy’s eyes flutter open and immediately begin stinging as powdery blue snow falls into them. She begins suffocating, flailing around in the snow in a desperate attempt to dig herself free. Her right arm feels like it’s been peeled down to the bone, then reassembled with only half the material. The screaming fingers reach her face and she feels the warm rush of blood as it soaks through the snow and into her nose and mouth. Someone grabs her and she’s yanked upwards into the sunlight where marines and medical personnel move in a frenzy in the area around the glowing green pool. The explosion seems to have reached all the way back to the larger group of scientists, or at least that’s as far as the blood has gotten. Comparatively, she appears to be one of the lucky ones. She observes one man who’s been cut in half, though his legs aren’t anywhere to be seen. She fondly recognizes the hair of another woman, though that’s about as much as can be seen of her. Other scientists are somehow unscathed, one of which is still squatting directly next to her, feverishly biting his nails while staring at the edge of the green pool. Once it’s her turn to be helped, painkillers are quickly administered, and she watches with a surprising disconnect as her arm is bandaged. The limb looks as if a shotgun loaded with coins were blasted into it, creating numerous large holes that cut straight through to the other side. She can’t move her fingers.

Hamhock sits alone within the chaos. Looking exhausted, he rests in the middle of the pool, lazily propping an elbow up on the handle of the pickaxe. The right half of his face sags down like it’s been anesthetized, the pupil of his right eye only barely poking out from under the lid. The pickaxe itself is what catches her attention though. The previously silver metal head is now flaking with rust, and the wooden handle looks nearly rotted through, the more intact parts growing with thick green moss. He has no attention for that though, instead only staring down at the small hole he put in the ice. Green and purple gas steadily leaks from the opening, and dancing within the fumes she can see a handful of small shards, each about the size of a fingernail. Gravity, nor the wind, appear to have any affect on them. Uncanny doesn’t begin to describe it. They almost look superimposed, existing in a world completely separated from scene around them, if not everything altogether.

She approaches him and he looks up only briefly when he hears the marines behind him level their guns at her. She sits cross legged on the ice, watching the display with him for a long while.

“Your terms?” She asks.

He doesn’t say anything for several minutes, after which he slowly lifts his gaze up to her. At this point he still doesn’t say anything, and she begins to get the feeling that his eyes are still trying to piece together what’s directly in front of him.

“Hm?” Is his first word, spoken like he hasn’t talked to anyone in several days.

“Your terms?” She asks again, her legs growing cold against the ice.

“Ah, you’re the spy.” He says, relaxing a little to stare back down at the hovering shards. “They… Well I’m sure you know but… No one thought it would.” Guilt isn’t a good look on him, so he quickly tries to shrug it off. “Well maybe if the team had all been *actual* geologists they might’ve figured out that this was a shit idea.” He tosses her a small smile before taking a deep breath and adjusting himself on the ice.

“I have a thought for you:” He says next. “Let’s say you have a room, with… twenty people, for arguments sake. And at some point these people are going to figure out how to build a gun. It’s bound to happen one way or another, all the materials exist, so at some point someone’s going to figure out how to put them together in the right order and… Bang!” He mimes a finger gun and fires it into the little hole. “Now let’s say that instead of being a shitty spy, you are god. Go you… As god, you have a tough choice: whose divine destiny will it be to create the first gun? Now think long and hard about this one, because as god you know that eventually, any old fuck will be able to cobble something together. But the first person will forever be the one that sets the stage, and… for a time, they will have absolute power. Being god, you can create any person you want, so I want to know who your champion would be, the one that will create the most peace for the longest time after the firearm comes into existence.” The spy looks at him blankly so he quickly adds: “Broad strokes, occupation, temperament, whatever.”

Her mind stays blank for a while, until a reasonable answer floats in out of nowhere and she says:

“The favorite. The one that everyone adores.”

“Good pick.” He smirks. “Thing is, I don’t know how long someone can remain everyone’s darling once they have such a power. Say a man’s son is murdered and he wants your champion to do what’s right, introduce some lead justice right into the murderers lung. Do you think, no matter how that turns out, the favorite will still be *everyone’s* favorite?

“The leader then.” The spy says. “The guy that everyone looks at and says ‘Yup. That’s our boy.’”

“There you are.” Hamhock claps his hands a few times. “Now *that* will bring some peace.”

“But?” The spy asks, annoyed by the smug expression on his face.

“But it can’t last.” He shrugs. “Leaders lead to something, then once they have it they inevitably lead *against* something. An empire always makes enemies at some point, and then poof, no more peace.”

“So then I imagine your answer is giving the gun to yourself?”

“How astute of you.” He chuckles. “Yes I would give it to the slimy, selfish businessman who loves no one but himself. He’s awful, terrible, he gets dozens of his own staff injured and killed for a publicity stunt. But you will never see him on a crusade, he will never go to war, because ultimately he knows that the way he will make a killing, is to ensure that he has as many customers as possible. The favorite can never have a super-weapon because they won’t use it and will be deposed, and the leader can’t have one because they have too much of a cause to use it. Business is terrible, it is abusive, it is cruel, it is heartless, and ultimately it is the bane of countless lives across this galaxy… But I look at this and I don’t see something that can be handled by someone who has feelings. Who knows what it’s capable of, by the way those lab coats are talking about my fuckup… I don’t think it’s going to be a very simple answer.” He sighs. “I look at the Federation and I… They are *everything* that we should be doing, the right thing through and through. The unification of multiple alien civilizations, all with a seat at the table as they guide the galaxy into the brightest possible future. So tell me… spy, would you put this rock into a room with *all* of them in it? Or would you rather turn the other way and let me piss it away, puttering my little ships around to turn a buck? Because those are my terms. This stuff? It’s ours. All of it, forever, and you’re going to help me keep it that way. I don’t care if there’s another planet out there somewhere where they worship this stuff. I don’t care if they’re the cutest little shits in the universe, I expect you Feds to roll up with a big governmental grin and say ‘don’t worry, we’ll take it from here,’ before tossing me the fuckin keys.“ He laughs. “We saw how nuclear weapons went, ‘mutually assured destruction,’ fattest load of shit I’ve ever seen. Hell, what’s to say I’m better than them, what’s to say my successor doesn’t get a big head and fuck it all up for everybody.”
